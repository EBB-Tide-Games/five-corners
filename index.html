SCORING

Every number, and where it comes from

FLOW is the whole game. Clear a car within 18 seconds of it arriving and your streak grows. Every four clean clears raises the multiplier: x2, x3, x4. Nearly everything below is multiplied by it, so the difference between x1 and x4 is the difference between a decent shift and a great one.

Go 2.6 seconds without clearing anybody and the streak starts bleeding away. A collision, a meltdown or a missed ambulance wipes it out entirely.

Ambulance and high water pay in FLOW, not points. Get the ambulance through in time, or keep Beach Road moving while it floods, and you gain +6 flow — enough to jump a whole multiplier. Fail the ambulance and you lose the streak.

Stars rate the shift you just played, not the day. Each shift has its own ladder, and the harder shifts sit lower — they pay less for the same work, so they ask for less. Points buy the first four stars. The fifth is a perfect shift: the five-star score, finished, no collisions, all three goals. Your score carries across the day, and finishing every shift earns a rating for the whole day.

Three collisions, or a junction jammed solid, ends the shift.

ABOUT

Five Corners · Vineyard Haven, Massachusetts

The place is real. The game is not. Five streets meet at one point in the middle of Vineyard Haven, on Martha’s Vineyard: Water Street, Beach Street, Beach Street Extension, Beach Road and Lagoon Pond Road. It has been known simply as Five Corners for generations, and Islanders have always got through it on courtesy and patience.

It sits a short walk from the ferry terminal, so arrivals and departures set the rhythm of the whole junction. Low-lying ground nearby means Beach Road is no stranger to standing water after a storm.

Everything past this point is invented — the ninety-second shifts, the scoring, the hazards and the crossing guard. The layout has been simplified and rearranged to make a game of it.

Your job. Ninety seconds a shift. Tap a road to wave its traffic through — only one runs at a time, and the green lapses in a few seconds, so you are never allowed to stop deciding.

Hold your nerve. Press and hold the open road and the guard waves hard: cars come faster and closer together, worth more points — but they clog the junction behind them, and the guard’s arm only lasts a couple of seconds before it needs to recover. The ring around the guard shows what is left. Tap the guard to throw everything to red.

Then the island fights back. Yellow cars have a boat to catch. People waiting at the crosswalk will not wait forever, so hold the road before they set off. An ambulance needs a clear path. Water rises on Beach Road and slows it to a crawl. And the game is built so that all of it lands at once, right as the ferry sails.

Clear cars promptly and FLOW climbs to a x4 multiplier. Let one driver sit too long and you lose the lot. Three collisions ends your shift — and switching roads while cars are still crossing is exactly how collisions happen.

Keep the island moving.

An affectionate work of fiction, made by someone fond of the Island. Street names are used to evoke a well-loved place. Nothing here describes real traffic conditions, real right-of-way, or how to drive, walk or cycle anywhere.
